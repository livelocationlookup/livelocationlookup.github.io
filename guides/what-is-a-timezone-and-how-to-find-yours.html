What Is a Timezone and How Do You Find Yours?

Timezones exist because the Earth turns. As the planet rotates, different places face the sun at different times, so noon in Karachi is not noon in London or New York. To keep clocks sensible, the world is divided into zones, each roughly fifteen degrees of longitude wide, with local time set a fixed number of hours ahead of or behind Coordinated Universal Time, known as UTC. Pakistan Standard Time is UTC plus five hours, so when it is noon in London in winter, it is five in the afternoon in Karachi.

Most timezones are whole hours from UTC, but a few are offset by thirty or forty-five minutes — India is UTC plus five and a half hours, and Nepal is UTC plus five hours and forty-five minutes. Some countries also use daylight saving time, moving clocks forward an hour in summer and back in winter, which means the difference between two places can change during the year. Pakistan does not currently observe daylight saving, which keeps things simpler, but anyone scheduling calls with Europe or North America needs to watch for their clock changes in March and October.

Your phone and computer already know your timezone. They set it automatically from your location or your network, and they show it in the date and time settings. On a website, JavaScript can read your timezone directly from the browser — our timezone finder tool does exactly that, showing your timezone name and your offset from UTC without sending anything to a server. You can also find the timezone of any place from its coordinates, which is useful when planning travel or scheduling a call with someone abroad.

Why does this matter in daily life? Because a meeting set for three o'clock means nothing until everyone agrees which three o'clock. A flight landing time, a live broadcast, a deadline for an online form — all of these depend on timezones. Getting it wrong by one hour is a common and expensive mistake. The safest habit is to write times with the timezone attached, for example 15:00 PKT or 10:00 UTC, and to use a world clock app when you regularly talk to people in other countries.

Travellers notice timezones most sharply. Crossing one means your body clock is an hour out; crossing several is jet lag. Phones handle the change automatically when they connect to a local network, but it is worth double-checking that your calendar appointments shifted correctly, especially recurring meetings. Some calendar apps keep events in the timezone where they were created, which can cause confusion. Checking the timezone display before an important call takes ten seconds and saves an embarrassing missed meeting.

Timezones also shape how the internet works. Websites show you local times, shops open and close by their own local clock, and a sale that ends at midnight ends at a different moment for every customer. Knowing your own timezone, and how to find anyone else's, is a small piece of digital literacy that pays off constantly. Try our timezone finder to see your own offset, then look up the timezone of a city you would like to visit — the numbers will start to feel familiar very quickly.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I find my timezone?

Your phone shows it in date and time settings, and our timezone finder tool reads it straight from your browser.

Why is Pakistan UTC+5?

Pakistan sits about seventy-five degrees east of Greenwich, and each fifteen degrees of longitude is roughly one hour, giving an offset of five hours.

Do timezones change with daylight saving?

In countries that observe it, yes — clocks move forward an hour in summer. Pakistan does not currently observe daylight saving.
