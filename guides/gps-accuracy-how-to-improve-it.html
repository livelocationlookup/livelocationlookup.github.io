GPS Accuracy: Why It Goes Wrong and How to Improve It

GPS accuracy is usually good enough that we never think about it — until the blue dot jumps to the wrong street, or a running app adds a phantom kilometre to your route. Understanding why this happens makes it much easier to fix. Your phone works out its position by timing signals from satellites overhead. When it can see four or more satellites clearly, the fix is tight, often within five metres. When buildings, trees, or even heavy cloud block those signals, the phone falls back on weaker estimates and accuracy suffers.

The most common cause of poor accuracy is being indoors or surrounded by tall buildings. Satellite signals are radio waves, and they bounce off glass and concrete, arriving slightly late and confusing the calculation — an effect called multipath. A phone in a deep shopping mall or a narrow street between skyscrapers may be off by fifty metres or more. The fix is simple: step into open sky for a few seconds. Even a short walk to a window or a doorway can let the phone lock onto more satellites and snap the dot back to where you actually are.

Your phone also uses Wi-Fi and mobile towers to help. Wi-Fi positioning is very accurate in cities, because the phone recognises nearby networks whose locations are known. Turning Wi-Fi on — even without connecting to a network — usually improves accuracy indoors. Mobile towers are the roughest method, accurate to a few hundred metres at best, and they are mainly a fallback when nothing else is available. Keeping all three systems switched on gives the phone the most information to work with, which is why high accuracy mode exists in Android settings.

There are a few settings worth checking when accuracy is poor. First, make sure location services are on and set to high accuracy rather than battery saving. Second, check that the app in question actually has location permission, and that it is set to allow all the time or while in use rather than only once. Third, restart the app or the phone: a stale GPS fix can linger for minutes after you move. Fourth, check for a system update, because phone makers regularly improve positioning software.

Accessories and habits matter too. A thick metal phone case can weaken the signal slightly, and holding the phone deep inside a bag or a car glove box makes things worse. If you use your phone for running or cycling, wear it on your arm or mount it on the handlebars rather than in a pocket under layers of clothing. In a car, a windscreen mount near the glass gives a far better fix than a phone lying on the passenger seat, and it is safer as well.

Finally, keep your expectations realistic. Consumer GPS is designed for navigation, not surveying: five to ten metres outdoors is normal, and a couple of metres of drift while standing still is not a fault. If you need centimetre precision — for mapping a field or marking a boundary — you need specialist equipment. For everything else, open sky, Wi-Fi on, high accuracy mode, and a quick restart will solve the vast majority of problems, and our live location tool shows the accuracy figure your phone is reporting so you can see the improvement for yourself.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my location jump around while I stand still?

The phone keeps recalculating from slightly different satellite signals. A few metres of drift is normal; standing in open sky reduces it.

Does turning on Wi-Fi really help GPS?

Yes. Wi-Fi positioning is very accurate in built-up areas and helps a lot indoors where satellites are blocked.

Can weather affect GPS accuracy?

Heavy rain and thick cloud can weaken signals slightly, but the effect is small. Buildings and terrain matter far more.
