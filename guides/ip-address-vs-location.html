IP Address vs GPS Location: What Is the Difference?

Your devices have two very different ways of guessing where you are: your IP address and your GPS. They are often confused, but they work in completely different ways and give very different levels of accuracy. Understanding the difference explains why a website thinks you are in a different city, why a map app is precise, and why a VPN changes one but not the other.

An IP address is the number your internet connection uses to send and receive data. When you visit a website, the site sees your public IP address and can look it up in a database that maps IP ranges to rough locations — usually a city or a region. This is called IP geolocation. It is cheap and it works without any permission, which is why websites use it to show local weather, prices, or language. But it is only approximate: it typically points to the city where your internet provider's equipment sits, which can be tens of kilometres from your actual position.

GPS is entirely different. It uses satellite signals to calculate your exact position, usually within five to ten metres outdoors. It needs your permission, it works on your device rather than on a distant server, and it is not visible to websites unless you explicitly allow it. That is why a maps app knows which street you are on while a website only knows roughly which city you are in. GPS is precise; IP geolocation is a rough guess.

This difference is why a VPN changes what websites think. A VPN routes your traffic through a server in another country, so websites see that server's IP address and believe you are there. Your GPS, however, is unaffected — your phone still knows exactly where you are, and any app with location permission will still see your real position. That is why a VPN can unlock a video service in another country but cannot fool a ride-hailing app into thinking you are somewhere else.

Each method has its place. IP geolocation is good enough for choosing a language, showing nearby shops, or setting a default timezone. It is fast, it needs no permission, and it works on every device. GPS is needed for navigation, delivery tracking, fitness apps, and anything where a few metres matter. Good apps use both: an IP estimate instantly, then a GPS fix to refine it once you grant permission.

The next time a website gets your location wrong, you will know why. It is looking at your IP address, not your GPS, and your IP address only knows the rough area of your internet connection. If you want a site to know exactly where you are, you need to grant location permission — and if you want it not to know, a VPN only hides the rough guess, not the precise one. Both tools have their uses, and knowing which is which keeps you in control.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does a website think I am in the wrong city?

It is using your IP address, which points to your internet provider's equipment, not your exact position. The estimate can be off by a whole city.

Does a VPN hide my GPS location?

No. A VPN only changes your IP address. Apps with location permission still see your real GPS position.

Which is more accurate, IP or GPS?

GPS, by far. GPS is accurate to a few metres outdoors, while IP geolocation is usually only accurate to a city or region.
