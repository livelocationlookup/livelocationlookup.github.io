Travelling Across Timezones: Beat Jet Lag and Stay on Time

Crossing timezones is one of the stranger experiences of modern travel. You board a plane in the afternoon and land in the morning of the same day, or you lose a whole night somewhere over the ocean. Your body, meanwhile, keeps its own time, and the mismatch is jet lag: tired when you should be awake, wide awake at three in the morning. A little planning makes it much easier to handle.

Jet lag depends on direction. Flying east is harder than flying west, because your body finds it easier to stay up later than to fall asleep earlier. A good rule is that you recover about one timezone per day travelling west, and only about half a timezone per day travelling east. A six-hour eastward jump can take four days to fully shake off, while the same jump westward might take two. Keep this in mind when scheduling important meetings for the first days of a trip.

The best preparation starts before you fly. In the days before departure, shift your sleep by an hour towards your destination's time — earlier if flying east, later if flying west. On the plane, set your watch to destination time as soon as you board, and try to sleep or stay awake to match it. Drink water, avoid alcohol, and get up to walk around on long flights. When you land, get outside into daylight as soon as you can: sunlight is the strongest signal that resets your body clock.

Your phone handles the clock change automatically when it connects to a local network, but double-check it. Calendar appointments can be a trap: a meeting created in one timezone may display at the wrong hour after you land, especially if it is a recurring event. Before an important call, confirm the time with the other person in writing, with the timezone attached. There are a few practical tricks worth knowing. Eat meals at destination times, even if you are not hungry — meal times also help reset your clock. Avoid long naps on arrival day; a short nap of twenty minutes is fine, but a three-hour sleep in the afternoon will wreck your first night. If you need to be sharp the next morning, consider arriving a day early. And if you travel regularly, keep a world clock on your phone's home screen with the zones you visit most.

Timezones are a small price to pay for crossing the world in hours, and with a little preparation they need not ruin the first days of a trip. Shift your sleep before you fly, follow destination time from boarding, get sunlight on arrival, and double-check your calendar. Your body will catch up — and the view from the window makes the whole adjustment worthwhile.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is jet lag worse flying east?

Your body clock finds it easier to delay sleep than to advance it, so eastward travel, which shortens the day, is harder to adjust to.

How long does jet lag last?

Roughly one day per timezone crossed flying west, and up to two days per timezone flying east.

Does my phone change timezone automatically?

Yes, when it connects to a local mobile network or Wi-Fi. Always double-check important calendar events after landing.
