How to Drop a Pin and Share It with Anyone

Dropping a pin is the simplest way to share an exact spot with someone. No address needed, no long explanation, no getting lost. You press and hold on a map, a pin appears, and you send it. The other person taps it and their map opens at the same place. It works for a meeting point, a parking spot, a campsite, a house with no street name, or a great viewpoint you want a friend to find. If you have never done it, it takes about thirty seconds to learn.

On Google Maps, press and hold anywhere on the map until a red pin drops. A panel appears at the bottom with the coordinates and, if there is one, the nearest address. Tap Share, choose your messaging app, and send it. The recipient gets a link that opens in their maps app at the same spot. On Apple Maps, the steps are nearly identical: press and hold, tap the pin, then Share. On OpenStreetMap-based apps the gesture is the same, though the share option may be under a menu.

A dropped pin is more useful than an address in several situations. At a festival or a market, there is no address to give — a pin is the only way to say exactly where your stall is. In a car park, a pin marks the row you left your car in. On a walk, a pin marks the trailhead. And for a new home or a rural property, a pin is far more precise than a street name that may not exist on every map. If you run a small business from home, a pin on your contact page can be more helpful than a written address.

There are a few ways to make your pins more useful. First, add a label: Google Maps lets you save a pin with a name like Mum's house or Stall 12, so you can find it again later. Second, check the pin before you send it — zoom in and make sure it sits on the right building, not the road next door. Third, if the spot is hard to find, add a short note in your message: blue gate, second floor, next to the bakery. A pin plus one sentence beats a pin alone.

You can also drop pins in advance. Planning a trip? Drop pins for your hotel, the station, and the restaurant you booked, and save them to a list. When you arrive, they are all waiting for you, even offline if you downloaded the map. Planning a delivery route? Pins in order make a simple, effective plan. The map becomes your notebook, and the pins are the entries.

Sharing a pin is a small skill with outsized value. It removes ambiguity from meeting up, it works anywhere on Earth, and it takes less time than typing an address. Next time someone asks where you are, try dropping a pin instead of describing the area — you will both get there faster.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I drop a pin on Google Maps?

Press and hold on the map until a red pin appears, then tap Share to send the location link.

Does the other person need the same app?

No. A shared pin is a web link that opens in any browser or maps app.

Can I save pins for later?

Yes. Save a dropped pin with a label and it appears in your saved places, available whenever you need it.
