How to Find the Coordinates of Any Place on the Map

Sometimes you need the coordinates of a place you are not standing in: a hotel you booked, a trailhead you plan to visit, a field you want to mark, or a friend's new house. Getting coordinates for any place on Earth is easy once you know the trick, and it works in every major maps app. The method is nearly the same everywhere: find the place, drop a pin, and read the numbers.

In Google Maps, search for the place or long-press on the map to drop a pin. On a computer, right-click the pin and choose What's here? — the coordinates appear in a small panel. On a phone, tap the pin and the coordinates appear in the details at the bottom, or copy the share link, which contains them. In Apple Maps, press and hold to drop a pin, then swipe up on the panel to see the coordinates. In OpenStreetMap, right-click and choose Show address, or use the share button to get a link with the coordinates in it.

You can also work backwards: if someone sends you coordinates, paste them into the search box of any maps app. Type them as two numbers separated by a comma, latitude first — for example 24.8607, 67.0011 — and the map will jump straight to the spot. Our coordinates-to-map-link tool does the same thing and gives you links for both Google Maps and OpenStreetMap, so you can share the spot in whichever service the other person prefers.

There are a few details that catch people out. The first is order: latitude always comes first, and swapping the numbers sends you to a completely different place — sometimes in the middle of an ocean. The second is the minus sign: a negative latitude means south of the equator, and a negative longitude means west of Greenwich. Dropping a minus sign can move your pin to the opposite side of the world. The third is format: most apps expect decimal degrees, so if you have degrees, minutes and seconds, convert them first or use a tool that accepts both.

Coordinates are also useful for record-keeping. Photographers note the coordinates of a viewpoint so they can return in better light. Farmers record the corners of fields. Researchers mark the exact spot where a sample was taken. A simple note with two numbers is more durable than a description, because it never depends on a landmark that might change or a street name that might be renamed. Save important coordinates in a notes app with a short label, and they will be there when you need them.

Once you can find coordinates for any place, maps become a much more powerful tool. You can plan a route to a trailhead that has no address, send a precise meeting point to a group, or check the distance between two places before you travel. It is a thirty-second skill, and it is one of the most useful things you can learn about maps.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I get coordinates in Google Maps?

Long-press to drop a pin, then tap it. On desktop, right-click the pin and choose What's here? to see the coordinates.

What order do coordinates go in?

Latitude first, then longitude, separated by a comma. Swapping them points to a different place entirely.

Can I paste coordinates into any maps app?

Yes. Paste decimal coordinates into the search box of Google Maps, Apple Maps, or OpenStreetMap and it will jump to the spot.
